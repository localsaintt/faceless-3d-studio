00Summary and how to read this report

Bottom line: this is buildable on your own computer with mostly free software. Blender 5.2 LTS does all 3D work. It is driven by Python scripts that Claude Code writes and runs headlessly from a structured storyboard, and rendered with EEVEE. Audio uses local open-source TTS plus a licensed SFX library, captions and counters are added in a separate step, and FFmpeg assembles the final 1080×1920 video. Automated QC runs before anything reaches you for approval. The realistic monthly cost is $20–55, plus a one-time hardware upgrade only if your computer lacks a reasonable GPU.

What matters most: the playbook's own data, and the creators we could verify, both say the premise, hook and escalation decide results, not the render quality. The system should therefore make ideas cheap to test, and keep each video genuinely new so it doesn't trip the platforms' "unoriginal / inauthentic / mass-produced" rules.

How confident to be in this research:

  • This research ran in a cloud sandbox whose network allowlist blocked direct access to tiktok.com, youtube.com, blender.org, support.google.com, Meta pages and most vendor sites. Most findings therefore come from search-engine excerpts of those pages, not the pages themselves.
  • Each claim in the appendices carries a label:
    • VERIFIED means a cited source (excerpt) supports it.
    • SECONDARY means a third-party source.
    • INFERENCE means my reasoning.
    • UNVERIFIED means claimed but not confirmed.
  • Frame-by-frame analysis was only possible for one reference video, through a public GitHub repo containing its extracted frames. Prices and policies should be re-checked on official pages before any spending or launch.
  • The playbook's own performance numbers are self-reported marketing claims and are not treated as expected results.

What the playbook implies about production (inference, not stated)

  • Visuals in the screenshots: blocky low-poly humanoids (box torso, box head, no faces) in large crowds, semi-realistic sky/ocean (reads like Blender's Ocean modifier or a shader ocean, plus an HDRI sky), a real-world-ish pier and palms, cutaway soil cross-sections with rock texture, and a cartoon farm scene with a bearded farmer and dog (this one is a more detailed toon character, likely a stock or AI-generated rigged model).
  • Text: small all-caps label plus a large serif counter top-left ("GAS RELEASED 738,000,000 L"), italic serif subtitle lines in the lower third, and bold all-caps meme captions with a single highlighted yellow word for the zombie format.
  • "No graphics card" plus "your own computer renders it" means scenes are designed to render cheaply: simple geometry, few lights, no heavy volumetrics.

The ten transferable rules (my synthesis)

  1. One-sentence premise that passes the "then what?" test.
  2. Real, bright, crowded, recognisable place: the contrast between the normal place and the impossible change is the hook.
  3. Short, bounded impossibility (5–10 s) with long consequences.
  4. A beat every 2–3 s, each one a consequence, never random cuts.
  5. A persistent on-screen number (counter, clock, day count).
  6. An ending built to be argued about in the comments.
  7. A recurring line or element that rewards regulars, varied each time.
  8. Target audience defined up front (US units, US places).
  9. 61–80 s by design for TikTok monetisation; the extra time is story.
  10. Every upload new in set, opening shot, camera path, script and sound, including new relative to our own previous videos.

04How these videos are technically made

What the evidence shows:

  • The playbook says every video was built with Claude Code and rendered on the creator's own Mac/Windows computer with no graphics card.
  • A third-party recreation of @man_ia_c's style uses Three.js/WebGL rendered through a headless browser, with a Blender EEVEE alternative path, edge-tts voice, ASS subtitles for the HUD and FFmpeg assembly.
  • A Zack D. Films freelancer cites Blender + Cascadeur + After Effects + EmberGen.
  • Jared Owen uses Blender.
  • Several "secret bunker build" Shorts state they are AI-generated video, and @man_ia_c carries TikTok's AI label.

Inference: the niche runs on three production families.

Family How it works Pros Cons Who (likely)
Code-driven 3D (Blender Python, or Three.js) An LLM writes scene code from a storyboard; low-poly assets plus procedural geometry; offline render; FFmpeg Cheap, fast, deterministic, fully original, scales with automation Character acting is limited; needs an asset library Playbook authors, the man_ia_c recreation, likely many new TikTok what-if accounts

Our choice is family 1 (code-driven Blender), with targeted borrowing from family 2 (a proper rigged character and animation library) and optional, later use of family 3 only as a style pass over our own renders, never as the source of the video.

The typical shot recipe (code-driven):

  1. Load a set kit: terrain/ground, sky (HDRI or procedural Nishita sky), water (Ocean modifier), buildings and props from CC0 libraries.
  2. Scatter variation with Geometry Nodes (seeded), so each video's layout is new.
  3. Instance characters with offset animation cycles to form crowds.
  4. Animate the anomaly with procedural keyframes, shader effects and, only when needed, a baked simulation.
  5. Animate the camera from a move library: tracking, crane, orbit, crash zoom, with handheld noise.
  6. Render an EEVEE image sequence.
  7. Overlay the text system (hook title, HUD counter, captions).
  8. Build the sound design from cues (impacts, risers, ambience) and add the narration.
  9. Normalise loudness to −14 LUFS and export.

05Blender and Python automation (analysis)

Full detail and sources are in Appendix B.

Version: pin to Blender 5.2 LTS (released 14 Jul 2026, supported to Jul 2028). It is GPL and free for any use, and you own your renders. Relevant features:

  • Geometry Nodes bundles and closures (5.0).
  • EEVEE ray-tracing overhaul and node-based cloth/hair (5.2, experimental).
  • An ACES/HDR colour pipeline.

How Claude Code drives it:

  • Versioned scripts run as blender -b set.blend -P build_shot.py -- --shot s05.json.
  • Three stages: build (assemble the scene from the spec, save a .blend per shot for inspection), bake (any simulation to disk, with a fixed seed), render (EEVEE PNG/EXR sequence, with "placeholder + no overwrite" so a crash resumes instead of restarting).
  • A low-resolution preview render (540×960, few samples) is checked before the final.
  • Claude can look at the rendered stills (multimodal) and fix composition before the full render. This is the key quality loop.

What Python can control: effectively everything. Object creation and linking from libraries, Geometry Nodes inputs, materials and node trees, lights, world, cameras, constraints, keyframes and F-curve modifiers, drivers, NLA action strips, physics settings and bakes, render settings and output.

API traps (confirmed changes the agent must know about):

  • Blender 5.0 removed Action.fcurves; F-curves now live in action channelbags/slots.
  • Grease Pencil v3 changed its API.
  • 5.0 video output requires media_type='VIDEO'.
  • 5.2 changed Geometry Nodes modifier input access.
  • The EEVEE engine id is BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT in 4.2–4.5 and back to BLENDER_EEVEE in 5.x.

Most tutorials and model training data predate these, so the project needs a "Blender 5.2 API notes" file in its CLAUDE.md.

MCP servers: the community "blender-mcp" (≈32.7K stars, renamed "MCP for Blender") and Blender Lab's official MCP (5.1+) let Claude drive a running GUI Blender. They are useful for interactive look-development of kits and characters, but not for batch production, which should be plain headless scripts.

Headless EEVEE: EEVEE needs a GPU (it is OpenGL/Vulkan/Metal based). It works on Windows and macOS desktops. On a display-less Linux server it may need EGL or a virtual X server (Xvfb). Smoke-test it on your machine in Stage B; Cycles is the fallback.

Physics policy: fake by default (keyframes, noise modifiers, drivers, shader effects, the Ocean modifier needs no bake). Simulate only when the motion is the hook, such as building collapse (Cell Fracture + rigid body), splashes (particles; Mantaflow rarely) or cloth (flags, towels). Always bake to disk with a pinned seed before rendering.

Camera automation: a library of parameterised moves maps storyboard words to code:

  • establishing_wide, crash_zoom, track_alongside, crane_up, orbit_reveal, top_down_snap, telephoto_static, pov_handheld.
  • Implemented with Follow Path / Track To / Damped Track constraints, keyframed focal length and DOF, and a noise F-curve modifier for handheld shake.
  • Composition rules (subject in the upper-middle third of a 9:16 frame, clear of the platform UI) are applied automatically and checked in QC.

Three.js verdict: deterministic capture is possible but fragile, and it would duplicate the asset pipeline. Use it later for instant browser previews or an approval page, not for final renders. Blender renders every final frame.

Post-production:

  • FFmpeg (free) for concat, overlays, ASS subtitles with animated captions and counters, and two-pass loudnorm to −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP.
  • WhisperX / faster-whisper for word-level caption timing.
  • Not recommended for automation:
    • DaVinci Resolve: scripting needs the paid Studio version.
    • CapCut: no API, and its terms reportedly prohibit automation.
    • MoviePy v2: breaking API changes.
  • Remotion is a good alternative for animated text (free for individuals and companies of 3 or fewer).
  • Blender's VSE is possible but slower than FFmpeg for this.

Recommendation: "Living Diorama". Semi-real, sun-drenched environments shot like a miniature model world (subtle tilt-shift depth of field, slightly saturated colour, crisp shadows) populated by our own stylised characters.   • The miniature look hides low-poly simplicity (viewers read it as a deliberate toy-world), stays readable at phone size, and is cheap in EEVEE (depth of field + bloom + AO).
  • The playbook's rule 2 (bright, busy, famous) is native to the look.
  • The rounded character design is original, quick to rig once (one Rigify or Mixamo-compatible skeleton), and easy to vary by palette and shape keys.
  • It extends to all four formats: diorama cutaways suit "How it's built", map/isometric views suit survival, and a planet-as-diorama suits long-form.

House style guide v1 (proposal)

  • Characters: 1 base mesh, ~2–4k tris; 5 body-shape keys (tall/short/broad/kid/elder); 12 outfit palettes; dot eyes + optional brow decal for expressions (worried, shocked, happy) via swappable textures. No mouths by default (cheap, and it avoids lip-sync); a mouth decal only for close-ups. Shared skeleton so one animation library drives everyone.
  • Environments: real-place silhouettes (not exact replicas): recognisable skyline cues, colours and landmarks via signage and generic shapes. Clean PBR materials with simplified textures (sand, concrete, water, glass, grass), all from CC0 libraries or procedural.
  • Palette: daylight base of sky-blue, sea-teal, sand-warm, palm-green; a single event colour (signal magenta) reserved for the impossible thing (sound rings, the anomaly, the "change") so viewers learn: magenta = what changed. That recurring visual rule is part of the brand.
  • Lighting: noon sun at 45–60°, soft shadows, light AO, a gentle bloom; never night as the main setting (playbook data). Golden hour only for the ending.
  • Camera: every video opens with a low wide establishing shot of a famous place (the first frame must read as "a real place" in 0.5 s). Then aerial/diorama top-down, tracking shots at human height, and an orbit reveal for the finale. 24–35 mm for action, 85–135 mm "telephoto miniature" for scale gags. A light handheld noise layer on every camera.
  • Animation: snappy and slightly exaggerated (squash/stretch on impacts), crowds desynchronised with per-instance offsets.
  • Text: top-left HUD counter (small caps label + large tabular-figure number) in a distinctive monospace/grotesk pairing; centred lower-third captions, 2–4 words at a time, max 2 lines, safe-area aware (keep clear of the bottom ~20% and right ~15% where TikTok UI sits). Our own fonts, not the playbook's serif italic.
  • VFX: stylised, readable (rings, cones, particles, debris chunks) rather than photoreal sims.
  • Audio identity: a short original 3-note "anomaly" sting when the change happens; no stock music beds.

11Example storyboard (original): "What if sound moved slower than you can run?"

One-line test: "What if sound suddenly moved slower than you can run, for 10 seconds, on Miami Beach?" The natural response is "wait, then what happens?", so it passes. Why it fits the rules: one everyday thing (sound); a bright, busy, famous place (South Beach / Ocean Drive); a live counter (speed of sound, sonic booms); a strong comment hook ("you'd hear your own scream after you"); a sequel ("…what if light was this slow?"). Sound is invisible, so the visual signature becomes visible sound rings: translucent expanding shells that crawl slowly through the air. It's cheap to render, unusual, and easy to brand. Premise logic (for accuracy): normal speed of sound in air ≈ 767 mph (343 m/s). In this world it drops to 7 mph (≈3.1 m/s), which is jogging pace. Consequences: speech arrives seconds late; anything faster than 7 mph (cars, bikes, jet skis, a sprinting kid, a thrown ball) outruns its own sound and drags a sonic boom (a shock cone, like a jet passing Mach 1). When the 10 seconds end, all the delayed sound still in flight is the payoff. Target length: 72 s (above the 61 s Creator Rewards floor) at 30 fps = 2,160 frames, 1080×1920. Running element (ours): an ice-cream truck whose jingle is always late. In this episode it arrives 40 seconds after the truck has left. It can recur across episodes, done differently each time.

sets.*.kit + seed A set kit is a .blend library (terrain, road, building, palm and umbrella pieces) linked with bpy.data.libraries.load. Text / counters Not baked into the 3D render. Exported as a timeline (overlays.json) and composited afterwards so wording can change without re-rendering 3D.

12.3 Minimal illustrative bpy (for understanding, not production code)

Keyframing a constraint property like this works in 5.x. Writing F-curves directly must use the 5.0+ channelbag API (see §5).

import bpy, math, sys, json
args = sys.argv[sys.argv.index("--") + 1:]
shot = json.load(open(args[0]))
scn = bpy.context.scene
scn.render.engine = "BLENDER_EEVEE"        # 5.x id ("BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT" in 4.2–4.5)
scn.render.resolution_x, scn.render.resolution_y = 1080, 1920
scn.render.fps = 30
scn.frame_start, scn.frame_end = 1, int(shot["duration"] * 30)

car = bpy.data.objects[shot["actors"][0]["asset"]]
path = bpy.data.objects[shot["actors"][0]["path"]]
fp = car.constraints.new("FOLLOW_PATH"); fp.target = path; fp.use_fixed_location = True
fp.offset_factor = 0.0; fp.keyframe_insert("offset_factor", frame=1)
dist = shot["actors"][0]["speed_mph"] * 0.447 * shot["duration"]
fp.offset_factor = min(1.0, dist / path.data.splines[0].calc_length())
fp.keyframe_insert("offset_factor", frame=scn.frame_end)

cam = bpy.data.objects["Camera"]
trk = cam.constraints.new("DAMPED_TRACK"); trk.target = car; trk.track_axis = "TRACK_NEGATIVE_Z"
scn.render.filepath = f"//renders/{shot['id']}/frame_"
bpy.ops.render.render(animation=True)

16Risks and limitations

  • Content risk > tech risk: the playbook's own data says the premise decides results. The pipeline must make it cheap to test many ideas, not perfect one.
  • Originality flags: a templated pipeline naturally produces sameness. Enforce per-video uniqueness (new set seed, new opening composition, new camera path, new script, new sound design) and keep a reuse ledger.
  • Character animation quality is the hardest visual problem (contacts, hand-object interaction). Mitigate with the stylised design, avoiding close hand interactions, and the animation library.
  • Physics sims (water, destruction) are slow and non-deterministic. Prefer procedural/keyframed fakes and bake any sim.
  • Licensing drift: asset/tool licences change. Keep a licence field on every asset and avoid "free for personal use" sources.
  • Platform policy drift: monetisation rules (TikTok 1-minute rule, YouTube inauthentic content, Meta unoriginal content) change often. Re-check before launch.
  • Accuracy: what-if science must be plausible. Add a fact-check step and label estimates.
  • Hardware: without a decent GPU, EEVEE renders slow down. Measure on your machine in Stage B before promising throughput.
  • Real places: use real place names and generic recreations; avoid trademarked logos, real businesses' signage and real people.
  • Revenue uncertainty: creator-fund income is volatile and audience-dependent (see the playbook's $4.43 at 1.4M views).
  • This environment: this cloud session is a temporary container (no GPU, and files are lost unless committed). Production should run on your own computer, with the project in its own git repository.

Method and limits (read first)

  • WebFetch was blocked for every host tried (tiktok.com, youtube.com, ladbible.com, wikipedia.org returned ENOTFOUND, meaning the session's network allowlist blocks them). No profile page, video page, or article was opened directly.
  • Evidence therefore comes from WebSearch result snippets, which a search model summarises. I treat a snippet as VERIFIED only when it quotes a specific URL, a caption, or a number tied to a source. Snippet numbers can be stale or cached.
  • The WebSearch budget ran out (200 calls per turn, shared by all agents) before I could search Animagraffs, more zombie-scenario channels, and the Zack D. Blender tutorial contents. More searches need a follow-up turn.
  • One primary artifact was obtained: a public GitHub repo, zNightmare15/what_if_project (cloned via git, last commit 2026-10-25). It is a third-party reverse-engineering of the @man_ia_c electrons video. It contains frames extracted from the original TikTok, which carry the TikTok watermark, @man_ia_c, and TikTok's "AI-generated" label. It also contains the original audio, with TikTok metadata and a duration of 80.09 s. I viewed those frames directly. Claims based on the frames are VERIFIED (frames). The repo author's timing measurements are third-party measurements.
  • TikTok video IDs encode the posting time (id >> 32 = Unix seconds). Dates marked "ID-decoded" come from that method.
  • Labels: VERIFIED = backed by the cited URL or artifact. INFERENCE = my reading or extrapolation, not confirmed. UNVERIFIED = claimed somewhere (e.g. the playbook PDF) but not confirmed.

Cross-cutting findings

What appears to drive retention (evidence quality noted)
  1. Hook in 0–3 s. Third-party vendor blogs only; there is no official YouTube data, and YouTube does not expose swipe-away directly.
    • Claimed benchmarks: <30% swipe-away in the first 3 s = strong hook; ~80% retention at 3 s as a target; "~70% decide in first 2 s" (attributed to TikTok, unsourced). Examples: bytecap.io, quso.ai and shortsfast.com blogs.
    • Treat all of these as rules of thumb.
    • VERIFIED exemplar: man_ia_c puts the full question on screen at frame 0 with one highlighted keyword and starts a countdown immediately.
  2. Countdown / telemetry counters. VERIFIED in man_ia_c: an ms-precision countdown, "time slowed 11×", and a live "EARTH IS NOW X MILES WIDE" counter. VERIFIED via article in lunarcheeseee: an mph countdown from 1,037 with named thresholds (1,028, 800). INFERENCE: counters create an implicit "watch until it hits zero" contract.
  3. Escalation ladder plus a twist ending. VERIFIED in man_ia_c: park → hands → people → planet → Moon → "nothing left to come back to". Lunarcheeseee escalates by speed thresholds.
  4. POV / second person ("You're in Central Park…"). VERIFIED in captions (man_ia_c, moon2animation, #pov tags).
  5. Comment-bait and serialization. VERIFIED: "Which one next? 👇" (man_ia_c), "Would you tell them?" and Part 1/Part 2 (the_whatif_animator), "You asked for it (4,142 of you)" (The What If Studio).
  6. Topic recycling. VERIFIED: "Earth stops spinning" (Zack D 2025 → lunarcheeseee 2026). Electrons-vanish was cloned within 2 days.
  7. Long for a "short". VERIFIED: the man_ia_c video is ~80 s. TikTok rewards longer completions. INFERENCE: these are not 15 s clips.

2. Headless automation

CLI pattern
  • VERIFIED blender -b file.blend -P script.py -- arg1 arg2. -b means background (no UI). -- stops Blender's argument parsing, and the script reads its own arguments with sys.argv[sys.argv.index('--')+1:]. Arguments run in order, so set -o, -s/-e, -F before -f/-a. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/2.80/advanced/command_line/arguments.html , https://archive.blender.org/wiki/2015/index.php/Reference/CL , https://salaivv.notion.site/Blender-Python-Cheatsheet-9cfb88dbc4a34d829d4305982053a05e
  • VERIFIED --cycles-device CPU|CUDA|OPTIX|HIP|ONEAPI|METAL with optional +CPU for hybrid rendering. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/gpu_rendering.html
  • INFERENCE (standard practice):
    • Use --factory-startup so user prefs and add-ons don't make runs non-reproducible. Use --python-exit-code 1 so a Python exception gives a non-zero exit code the agent can detect. Use --log-level / -noaudio as needed.
    • Split jobs into build (script → .blend saved with bpy.ops.wm.save_as_mainfile), bake (sims → disk cache) and render (blender -b scene.blend -a). Each stage is then cacheable and restartable, and the agent can inspect the .blend.
bpy building blocks (INFERENCE, API stable since 2.8 except where noted)
  • Objects: bpy.data.meshes.new + bpy.data.objects.new + collection.objects.link (preferred over bpy.ops in background mode, because ops depend on context). Primitives: bpy.ops.mesh.primitive_*_add work headless.
  • Materials: mat = bpy.data.materials.new(name); mat.use_nodes = True (INFERENCE: use_nodes may be on by default / deprecated in 5.x), then edit mat.node_tree.nodes['Principled BSDF'].inputs['Base Color'].default_value.
  • Lights and cameras: bpy.data.lights.new(name, type='AREA'|'SUN'|'POINT'|'SPOT'), bpy.data.cameras.new, scene.camera = cam_obj.
  • Keyframes: obj.location = (...); obj.keyframe_insert('location', frame=f). This high-level call still works on 5.x and creates the slotted action automatically. For direct F-curve editing use the channelbag API (section 1). Set interpolation through kp.interpolation = 'BEZIER'|'LINEAR'|'CONSTANT' and easing with kp.easing.
  • F-curve modifiers: fc.modifiers.new('NOISE') with .strength, .scale, .phase for procedural shake and wobble; 'CYCLES' for loops.
  • Drivers: fc = obj.driver_add('rotation_euler', 2); fc.driver.expression = 'frame/10'. Simple expressions run without "Auto Run Python Scripts". INFERENCE: complex Python expressions need --enable-autoexec in headless runs.
  • Render: scene.frame_start/frame_end/frame_step, scene.render.fps, resolution_x=1080, resolution_y=1920, resolution_percentage, scene.render.filepath='//out/frame_####', then bpy.ops.render.render(animation=True) (or -a on the CLI).
  • Output format:
    • Recommended: PNG (or EXR) sequence, then FFmpeg assembly. A crash only loses one frame. render.use_overwrite=False + render.use_placeholder=True lets you resume and run several machines on one frame range. The colour pipeline stays clean.
    • Direct video on 5.x: image_settings.media_type='VIDEO' (VERIFIED field), file_format='FFMPEG', scene.render.ffmpeg.format='MPEG4', codec='H264'. Use it only for quick previews.
  • Validation: the agent should render one still (-f N) at 25–50% resolution and look at it (Claude can read PNGs) before launching a full animation run.
EEVEE headless: needs a GPU, and the display question is OS-specific
  • VERIFIED (historical): in 2.8x EEVEE needed an OpenGL context. Blender devs said "there must always be a display even for background rendering". Workarounds were Xvfb (very slow, software GL) or a headless Xorg on NVIDIA. Cycles CUDA/OptiX never needed X. https://devtalk.blender.org/t/blender-2-8-unable-to-open-a-display-by-the-rendering-on-the-background-eevee/1436 , https://developer.blender.org/T61164 , https://gist.github.com/UuuNyaa/f57aa37fc6ed2fbd5c166513a16a40e5
  • VERIFIED (single-source, current): a 2026 project reports Blender 5.2 running blender -b with EEVEE rendering through EGL with no DISPLAY/WAYLAND_DISPLAY on Linux. https://github.com/miyabi-sunny-side/blender-live
  • Conflicting (vendor): a 2026 render-farm vendor article claims EEVEE Next still needs a display context, and that headless SaaS boxes crash or render black. https://irendering.net/eevee-next-headless-dilemma-cloud-render-farm-guide/
  • INFERENCE / recommendation:
    • Linux + NVIDIA: current Linux builds create an EGL context when no display is present, so EEVEE works headless on a real GPU with the proprietary driver. Run a 1-frame smoke test on any new box or cloud image. Fallback: Xvfb + VirtualGL, or a headless Xorg with the NVIDIA driver.
    • macOS (Apple Silicon): EEVEE uses Metal and blender -b works from Terminal/SSH. Stay logged in to a GUI session and disable sleep; launchd jobs without a user session are less reliable.
    • Windows: works from a normal user session. Avoid Windows Services and Session 0, which may not have GPU access.
    • CPU-only: EEVEE needs a GPU. Software rasterization (Mesa llvmpipe) is technically possible but impractically slow and flaky. CPU-only boxes should use Cycles CPU.
Cycles devices
  • VERIFIED CUDA and OptiX (NVIDIA; OptiX needs compute capability ≥5.0 and driver ≥535), HIP (AMD RDNA1+), oneAPI (Intel Arc A/B), Metal (Apple Silicon, macOS 13+). No CUDA toolkit install is needed because kernels ship precompiled. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/cycles/gpu_rendering.html
  • INFERENCE: in headless runs, set devices in the script, because --factory-startup resets prefs: prefs = bpy.context.preferences.addons['cycles'].preferences; prefs.compute_device_type='OPTIX'; prefs.get_devices(); [setattr(d,'use',True) for d in prefs.devices]; scene.cycles.device='GPU'.
bpy as a pip module
  • VERIFIED pip install bpy gives "Blender as a Python module". 5.0.0 wheels target CPython 3.11; 5.1.x wheels target CPython 3.13. Wheels exist for Win x64/ARM64, manylinux x86-64 and macOS ARM64. Only the current LTS window stays on PyPI; older ones are on download.blender.org. https://pypi.org/project/bpy/ , https://pypi.org/project/bpy/5.0.0/
  • VERIFIED fake-bpy-module-5-x provides type stubs only, useful for IDE and agent autocomplete. https://pypi.org/project/fake-bpy-module-5-1/
  • INFERENCE: bpy in-process is handy for asset inspection and tests. For renders, prefer the blender binary in a subprocess: it isolates crashes, supports per-job timeouts, gives an exact version pin and is easier to parallelize. You must match the Python version exactly.
MCP servers
  • VERIFIED The community blender-mcp by ahujasid, renamed "MCP for Blender" / mcp-for-blender in Sep 2026, is MIT licensed with about 32.7k stars as of 30 Oct 2026. It works through a Blender add-on that opens a localhost socket plus an MCP server, and integrates Poly Haven and Hyper3D Rodin. Blender must be running (GUI) or tool calls fail. Its tool definitions use about 6.5k tokens of context. It is not affiliated with the Blender Foundation. https://aiwiki.ai/wiki/blender_mcp , https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp/releases , https://ai-heartland.com/tool/blender-mcp-guide/
  • VERIFIED (secondary) The official Blender Lab MCP server lives at projects.blender.org/lab/blender_mcp. It needs Blender ≥5.1, reached v1.0.3 on 11 Sep 2026, and is aimed at analysis, debugging and scripting rather than generative art. https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/official-blender-mcp-server-comparison-2026
  • INFERENCE: usefulness for this pipeline.
    • MCP is good for interactive look-dev: the agent pokes a live scene, takes viewport screenshots and iterates on lighting and layout with a human watching.
    • It is a poor fit for batch production. It needs a running GUI Blender, the state is opaque and lives in memory, and it is hard to reproduce, version or parallelize.

  • INFERENCE Falling and tumbling: rigid bodies (bpy.ops.rigidbody.object_add(), type='ACTIVE', mass, friction) plus bpy.ops.ptcache.bake_all(bake=True). 6. Physics

  • Rigid body (Bullet). INFERENCE: set up through bpy; scene-level scene.rigidbody_world with substeps_per_frame and solver_iterations; cache through point_cache.frame_start/end. Bake headless with bpy.ops.ptcache.bake_all(bake=True) (bakes all point caches), then save the .blend so the bake travels with it. Context: ptcache ops may need a context override in background mode, e.g. with bpy.context.temp_override(scene=scene, ...).
  • Cell Fracture. VERIFIED: extension v0.2.1 (above). INFERENCE: run once in the build stage (bpy.ops.object.add_fracture_cell_objects(...) after enabling the extension), then rigid-body the shards. Quality quirks: reviewers report pieces left unsplit. Alternative: a GN Voronoi fracture node group you control.
  • Mantaflow (smoke, fire, liquid). VERIFIED: "Bake All" works only with the Final cache type and cannot be paused or resumed; Modular and Replay can be resumed. https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/physics/fluid/type/domain/cache.html
    • INFERENCE: in headless runs use bpy.ops.fluid.bake_all() with a context override on the domain object, a fixed cache_directory, and an explicit cache_frame_start/end. Liquids at usable resolution (128–256) take tens of minutes to hours per shot and gigabytes of cache. Results are reproducible on the same build and hardware, but not byte-identical across machines or versions.
  • Cloth. INFERENCE: classic cloth modifier plus point cache works headless. 5.2's GN XPBD cloth and hair solver is experimental (VERIFIED, heise above), so avoid it in production in 2026.

  • When to fake vs simulate (INFERENCE): | Effect | Recommended | |---|---| | Object drops, bounces, settles | Rigid body sim, bake once | | Wobble, float, hover, shake, sway | Keyframes + Noise/Cycles F-modifiers, or drivers (deterministic, free) | | Ocean / water surface | Ocean modifier (procedural, no bake) | | Splash, pour, liquid interaction | Mantaflow only if it's the hero shot; otherwise stock VFX overlay in post | | Smoke/fire background | EEVEE volume with animated noise texture, or stock VFX plates; Mantaflow for hero shots | | Explosion debris | Cell Fracture + rigid body, or GN instanced debris with keyframed velocity | | Flags, cloth | Shape-key / GN wave deformer (fake) unless close-up |

  • Determinism and caching (INFERENCE):

    • Pin the Blender version (portable tarball, checksum).
    • Pin seeds (GN Random Value seed, particle seed, Cycles seed, use_animated_seed off).
    • Bake all simulations to disk in a dedicated "bake" stage and commit or archive the caches with the shot.
    • Never simulate inside the render loop.
    • Render nodes read caches only. Then re-renders after a camera tweak don't change the physics.

7. Cameras

  • INFERENCE (API long-stable):
    • Follow Path: c = cam.constraints.new('FOLLOW_PATH'); c.target = curve_obj; c.use_curve_follow = True. Animate curve.data.eval_time or c.offset_factor (with use_fixed_location=True) from 0→1 over the shot. Use path_duration for timing.
    • Track To / Damped Track: t = cam.constraints.new('TRACK_TO'); t.target = empty; t.track_axis='TRACK_NEGATIVE_Z'; t.up_axis='UP_Y'. Animate the empty for smooth reframing. Damped Track is less prone to flips.
    • Keyframed bezier moves: keyframe location/rotation_euler and set keyframe.interpolation='BEZIER' with easing for ease-in/out. Using a parent rig (empty "dolly" → empty "pan" → camera "tilt") keeps motion separable.
    • Camera shake: add a NOISE F-modifier to rotation (and lightly to location) F-curves of a shake empty parented between rig and camera. Strength ~0.005–0.02 rad, scale ~10–30 frames. Use phase to vary seeds per shot and use_restricted_range / blend in and out to ramp it.
    • Lens: cam.data.lens (mm), sensor_width, dof.use_dof, dof.focus_object, aperture_fstop. Vertical 9:16 means resolution_x=1080, resolution_y=1920; keep sensor_fit='AUTO' or set 'VERTICAL' explicitly.

  • Storyboard shot types → code (INFERENCE). The agent should implement each as a reusable function make_shot(type, subject, start, end, **params): | Shot | Implementation | |---|---| | Establishing / wide | Lens 18–24 mm, high position, slow dolly-in along a 2-point bezier, Track To scene centre | | Medium / close-up | Lens 35–50 mm (MS) or 85 mm (CU); distance = subject height × factor; Track To subject head bone or empty; DOF on subject | | Extreme close-up | 100–135 mm, small aperture f/1.8–2.8, micro-shake | | Dolly / push-in | Keyframe camera along its local −Z; ease in/out | | Truck / tracking | Follow Path on a curve offset parallel to the subject's path; same eval_time mapping as the subject | | Orbit | Camera parented to an empty at the subject; keyframe the empty's Z rotation (with linear interpolation for constant speed) | | Crane / boom | Animate parent empty Z plus tilt via Track To | | Whip pan / transition | 4–6 frame rotation keyframes plus motion blur (EEVEE and Cycles both support it) | | Handheld / POV | Noise F-modifier on a shake empty, stronger strength | | Drone / flyover | Follow Path along a long NURBS curve 20–100 m up, Track To terrain points | | Cuts | Separate camera objects + timeline markers bound to cameras (marker.camera = cam), which switch the active camera during -a |

  • INFERENCE: shot-list JSON is the contract. The storyboard becomes shots.json (id, type, duration_frames, subject, lens, move, notes). The agent builds one scene (or one .blend per shot), uses camera markers for cuts, and renders frame ranges per shot. Per-shot renders make it cheap to re-render only one shot.

Blender requirements (INFERENCE, from Blender's published requirements page; blender.org was unreachable this session)
  • Minimum: 64-bit quad-core CPU with SSE4.2 (x86) or Apple Silicon/ARM64, 8 GB RAM, 1080p display, GPU with 2 GB VRAM supporting OpenGL 4.3 (Vulkan 1.2 for the Vulkan backend). GPU drivers less than ~2 years old.
  • Recommended: 8+ core CPU, 32 GB RAM, GPU with 8 GB+ VRAM.
  • Cycles GPU support levels are VERIFIED in section 2.
A solid budget build for this workload (INFERENCE)
  • GPU: NVIDIA with 12–16 GB VRAM is the priority. VRAM caps scene size: crowds, 4K textures and EEVEE shadow pools. 8 GB will hit limits (VERIFIED that VRAM is the bottleneck on the RTX 4060: https://www.databasemart.com/blog/blender-gpu-cpu-benchmark-rtx4060-e5-2667v3).
    • Sweet spots: RTX 4070 Super/5070 (12 GB), RTX 4060 Ti 16 GB or 5060 Ti 16 GB (slower but more VRAM), used RTX 3090 24 GB (great VRAM per dollar).
    • NVIDIA wins because OptiX is the fastest Cycles backend and has the broadest headless Linux support.
  • CPU: 8–16 cores (Ryzen 7/9). It matters for scene building, sim baking (Mantaflow is CPU-only), FFmpeg encodes and WhisperX on CPU.
  • RAM: 64 GB recommended (32 GB minimum). Fluid bakes, big .blend files and running ASR, the browser and Blender together.
  • Storage: 2 TB NVMe for working data plus a big HDD or NAS for archive. One 2,100-frame PNG 1080×1920 sequence is about 4–8 GB (EXR multilayer ~20–60 GB); Mantaflow caches run into tens of GB.
  • OS: Linux (Ubuntu LTS) for headless reliability, with Docker images pinned to a Blender tarball. Windows works if you want the same box for Resolve.
  • INFERENCE: Apple Silicon vs NVIDIA.
    • M4 Max ≈ RTX 4070 in Cycles (VERIFIED score above) and has huge unified memory (36–128 GB), so very large scenes don't hit VRAM limits.
    • But it costs about 3–4x as much per unit of render throughput as a 4070 desktop, has no OptiX, and has no cloud parity: cloud farms are Linux/NVIDIA.
    • A Mac works well as the agent/dev workstation (EEVEE previews are fine). Use NVIDIA (local or cloud) for bulk renders.
Cloud rendering (INFERENCE: prices from memory of 2025–2026 listings; verify before buying)
  • SheepIt Render Farm: free, community-driven, credit/points system. You earn points by rendering others' frames and spend them on yours. Supports Cycles and EEVEE on recent Blender versions with a lag after each release. Limits: projects must be self-contained .blend files (packed textures), there are upload size and per-frame time limits, add-ons and Python are restricted, and the farm is public, so it is not suitable for confidential work. Good for occasional overflow; unreliable for deadlines.
  • GPU cloud (bring your own Blender in Docker):
    • RunPod: RTX 4090 about $0.35–0.70/h (community vs secure cloud), RTX 3090/A5000 about $0.20–0.45/h, L40S about $0.80–1.00/h.
    • Vast.ai marketplace: RTX 4090 about $0.25–0.60/h, RTX 3090 about $0.15–0.30/h; reliability varies by host.
    • Lambda / Paperspace / AWS g5/g6 are pricier (AWS g6.xlarge L4 about $0.80/h on-demand).
    • Example (INFERENCE): a Cycles short needing ~10 h on a 4070 is about 5 h on a 4090, so about $2–4 per full render on RunPod/Vast. EEVEE renders cost cents. Remember EEVEE headless on cloud Linux needs a test (section 2); Cycles OptiX is the safe cloud choice.
  • Commercial Blender farms (GarageFarm, RebusFarm, iRender, Ranch Computing, Fox Renderfarm): they bill per GHz-hour or per node-hour.
    • iRender rents whole GPU servers at about $1–9/h depending on the number of GPUs.
    • A typical short-form Cycles job costs about $10–100 depending on scene and priority.
    • They are convenient (managed Blender versions, add-on support), but for an agent-driven pipeline, raw GPU rental with your own pinned Docker image is cheaper and more reproducible.

Decision summary (INFERENCE unless noted)

  1. Pin Blender 5.2 LTS (VERIFIED, supported to Jul 2028) as a portable tarball or Docker image. Hold 5.3 until the pipeline is stable.
  2. The agent writes versioned bpy scripts and runs blender -b ... -P ... -- via Bash, in build → bake → render stages. MCP is optional, for interactive look-dev only.
  3. EEVEE is the default engine for vertical shorts: a 70 s short in about 1–3 h on an RTX 4070-class GPU. Cycles is the hero/upgrade path at about 5–18 h per short on the same card, or a few dollars on a rented 4090.
  4. Put a CLAUDE.md "5.x API gotchas" sheet in front of the agent:
    • Action.fcurves was removed (use channelbags);
    • GPv3 API changes;
    • media_type for video output;
    • 5.2 GN modifier input API change;
    • VSE strips rename;
    • engine id changes.
  5. Assets: CC0 first (Poly Haven, ambientCG, Kenney, Quaternius, MPFB2 output). Keep a license manifest. Mixamo is usable but unmaintained; download once, by hand. Ready Player Me is gone.
  6. Characters: standardize on one skeleton (Quaternius UAL, CC0); sequence clips with NLA; build crowds from time-offset instance variants or baked caches.
  7. Physics: fake with F-curve noise and drivers by default. Bake rigid body, cloth and fluids once to disk, then render from caches.
  8. Post: FFmpeg (concat, ASS karaoke captions, two-pass loudnorm to −14 LUFS), WhisperX or faster-whisper for word timing, optional Remotion (free only for companies with ≤3 people) or Motion Canvas (MIT) for motion graphics. No CapCut automation; Resolve's external API needs Studio.
  9. Three.js / Remotion-three for fast animatics and previs only; Blender produces the final pixels.
  10. Hardware: Linux box with an NVIDIA 12–16 GB GPU, 64 GB RAM and 2 TB NVMe, about $1.5–2.2k (INFERENCE). Burst to RunPod/Vast for Cycles.

4. Copyright basics for the pipeline

4.1 CC0 and CC-BY assets
  • CC0: no conditions and no attribution legally required; commercial use allowed. Credit is recommended as good practice. VERIFIED (official): https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/CC0_FAQ
  • CC BY 4.0: commercial use and adaptation allowed if you give appropriate credit, link the license, and indicate changes. Use TASL (Title, Author, Source, License). For video, credit in the description box is acceptable; title is optional for 4.0 but required for 3.0 and earlier. VERIFIED (official): https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/deed.en ; https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Recommended_practices_for_attribution
  • Avoid NC (non-commercial) and ND (no-derivatives) licenses for monetized videos. Inference from the standard CC license terms.
  • CC licenses cover copyright only. Trademark, publicity and privacy rights (for example a recognizable logo or a real person's likeness in a model) are not cleared. VERIFIED (official): https://creativecommons.org/reusing-cc-licensed-content/
  • Practice: keep an asset ledger (source URL, license, author, date downloaded) and put CC-BY credits in every video description on every platform.
4.2 Mixamo
  • Characters and animations are royalty-free for personal, commercial and non-profit projects, including films and video. Credit is not required. You may not redistribute the raw character or animation files, for example on asset stores. Mixamo content may not be used to train ML models. It is unavailable for Enterprise/Federated IDs and China-coded accounts. VERIFIED (official): https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html . Redistribution and credit details are VERIFIED (secondary: Adobe community forum answers quoting the FAQ).
  • Rendered video output is fine to monetize.
4.3 AI-generated assets and copyrightability
  • The US Copyright Office's Copyright and AI, Part 2: Copyrightability (2025-01-29) says AI outputs are protectable only where a human determined sufficient expressive elements: human-authored work perceptible in the output, or creative selection, arrangement or modification. Prompts alone are not enough. Using AI as a tool, or including AI material in a larger human work, does not bar protection for the human contributions. No legislative change was recommended. VERIFIED (official): https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf ; https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html
  • Implication: purely AI-generated elements (a raw AI texture or an unedited AI clip) are likely public domain in the US, so others could reuse them. Your human contributions (scripts, scene composition, edit timing, animation choices) are what protect the work. Keep project files as evidence of authorship. Also check each AI tool's terms of service for commercial-use rights; these vary by tool.
4.4 Real place names, landmarks and trademarked buildings
  • Copyright: 17 U.S.C. §120(a) says copyright in a building does not prevent pictures or "other pictorial representations" of it if it is in or ordinarily visible from a public place. A 3D rendition of a skyline or the Santa Monica Pier structure is very likely fine on copyright. VERIFIED (official statute): https://uscode.house.gov/ (17 USC 120)
  • Trademark: in Rock & Roll Hall of Fame v. Gentile Productions, 134 F.3d 749 (6th Cir. 1998), the court held that a photo of a famous building on a poster did not, without more, infringe the building's claimed trademark. VERIFIED (secondary/legal): https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/2515490/rock-roll-hall-of-fame-museum-inc-v-gentile-productions/
  • Place names (Miami Beach, Santa Monica Pier) used descriptively for the setting are generally low risk (nominative or descriptive use). UNVERIFIED (general legal principle; not legal advice).
  • Higher-risk elements: reproducing logos and signage precisely (the Santa Monica Pier arch sign is reportedly a city trademark the city licenses for commercial use; Pacific Park licenses its own imagery); implying sponsorship or endorsement; using a landmark name or logo in the channel name, thumbnails as branding, or merchandise; and a few landmarks with special rights (the Hollywood Sign is licensed and trademarked; the Eiffel Tower night lighting is copyrighted in France). Pier sign trademark: VERIFIED (secondary: Santa Monica Daily Press quoting a city attorney), https://www.smdp.com/city-hall-caught-in-sharknado/ . Hollywood Sign permits page: https://hollywoodsign.org/permits-filming
  • Mitigation: stylize or alter signage text, don't put landmark logos in branding or thumbnails, and add no "official" language. Filming permits apply only to physical filming, not to a CG rendition.

5. The playbook's 7 mistakes checked against official policy

# Playbook claim Verdict Basis
1 Uploading the same video twice Partially supported (strong on IG, YT and FB; indirect on TikTok CRP) Instagram is officially less likely to recommend reels already on Instagram. Facebook reduces distribution of detected duplicates. YouTube Spam policy targets high volumes of near-identical content, though "a few variations" are OK. TikTok's CRP FAQ doesn't name self-reuploads; TikTok Shop guidance says not to re-upload the same video. Cross-posting the same video to different platforms is not prohibited anywhere I found; just remove the other platform's watermark.
2 Copying another creator's script Confirmed in principle All three platforms treat content copied from others as unoriginal: TikTok Originality Policy, YouTube reused/inauthentic content, Meta unoriginal content. "Script" is not named explicitly, but a re-voiced copied script is copied content with minimal original input. Copying a script can also infringe copyright.
3 Reusing the same set, opening or ending Partially supported A consistent branded intro, outro or set is normal series branding; no policy bans it. The risk is when whole videos are templated: YouTube inauthentic content ("templated storylines", "generic templates giving the impression of mass production") and the Spam example (same music plus repetitive AI imagery across many videos). TikTok Shop warns about near-identical AI template output. Vary the story, camera and action enough that each video is clearly distinct.
4 Popular free music Partially supported TikTok: General Library music is not cleared for commercial or business use; the CML is. No official rule found that it disqualifies organic CRP videos. YouTube: any licensed track halves (or more) a Short's pool allocation; Shorts over 1 minute with a Content ID claim are blocked; "free" tracks may be in Content ID. The YouTube Audio Library is claim-free. Best option: original or owned music (or no-claim libraries).
5 Videos under 61 seconds don't earn Confirmed for TikTok CRP only TikTok requires "at least one minute" / "over a minute", so 61 seconds or more is a sensible buffer. Not true for YouTube Shorts (Shorts of any length up to 3 minutes share pool revenue by engaged views) or for Facebook.
6 Accounts that look like duplicates or impersonation Confirmed TikTok prohibits impersonation and ban evasion, and CRP excludes metrics from connected accounts. YouTube has an impersonation policy and extends enforcement across a person's channels. Meta removed more than 20M impersonating accounts in 2025. Multiple accounts are allowed if they are not deceptive. Use one clearly branded account per platform with a unique name, avatar and bio.
7 Judging or deleting too early; deleting during an appeal loses it Partially supported Officially, deleted or private TikTok videos are excluded from CRP and their rewards are not paid (Creator Academy), so deleting forfeits earnings, including any reinstated by a successful appeal. No official text says deletion voids the appeal itself; that part is a creator anecdote. "Don't judge too early" (videos picking up later) is a creator anecdote, not policy.
Extra: the playbook's "five non-original or low-quality flags in 30 days removes you from CRP"

Mostly confirmed, with corrections. Officially: "5 or more ineligible videos in the past 30 days" means the account "may be disqualified". The trigger counts any reason a video is ineligible, and removal is discretionary rather than automatic. Appeal account disqualification within 30 days.

Automation, bulk posting and multi-account notes
  • Official APIs for posting exist on both TikTok (Content Posting API, audited for public posting) and YouTube (Data API, audit recommended). Using them is allowed. What policies target is mass-produced, near-identical content, not the posting method.
  • Meta, YouTube and TikTok all police networks of accounts posting the same content (YouTube Spam "coordinated networks of channels", Meta duplicate reduction, TikTok "accounts acting in connection"). Do not run multiple same-platform accounts posting the same videos.
  • The same video posted once to each of TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and Facebook is fine. Upload clean masters without watermarks.
