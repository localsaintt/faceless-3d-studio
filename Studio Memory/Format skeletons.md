# Format skeletons

Beat structures per format, in our own words, built from measured references (Morrow Studio course data plus our own research). **Use the skeleton, never anyone's sentences, pictures, places, voice timing or catchphrases.** Every video needs its own script, place, look and ending line.

## Rules for every format
- 61–66 s, so TikTok Creator Rewards counts it (videos must be over 1 minute; check current terms).
- The full question or hook is readable in the first second (title card from frame 1).
- A new picture every 1.5–3 s. A short caption about every second.
- Narration density **3.2–3.8 words/s** (≈190–240 words for 61–66 s). Video 1 had 1.9 w/s, too sparse.
- A sound on most nouns that make one; music (if any) cuts out before the twist.
- The picture shows what the voice says, at the moment it says it.
- Our signature: the ice-cream truck line at the end (varied per video).

## What-if (3D)
| Beat | Share | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Title + hook | 0–3 s | The full question on screen; a famous, busy, daylit place by the water |
| The change | 3–8 s | One everyday thing changes, **for ~5 seconds**; a counter starts moving |
| Escalation ladder | 8–48 s | A new consequence every 2–3 s, growing in scale (person → street → city → planet); counters keep moving |
| Snap back / climax | 48–56 s | It returns; the biggest consequence lands |
| Aftermath + signature line | 56–65 s | Quiet beat, our running gag, a comment question |

## Zombie survival (Part 1, cliffhanger)
| Beat | Time (65 s) | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–4.5 s | Name the ordinary place + an order *not* to make the obvious move |
| Lead | 4.5–15 s | One person, a number for how busy the place is, why it could save them (with a condition) |
| Wrong move | 15–30 s | An alarm; the crowd makes the obvious move; a hard "no"; the math of the trap |
| Inside advantage | 30–40 s | A part of the place only the lead knows + one real, **sourced** survival rule |
| Supplies | 40–51 s | Real numbers + **an animal with a running joke** (drives comments) |
| Twist | 51–62 s | Survival looks easy, a beat of silence, then a 6–8-word reveal |
| End card | 62–65 s | "Part 2" + a choice question |
Reference measures: 145–185 words at 3.2–3.8 w/s; new shot every 1.5–2 s. Morrow does this in flat 2D; we do it in our 3D diorama style (isometric map views with labels suit it).

## How it's built (3D cutaway)
| Beat | Share | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | first line | The starting point and what it becomes; first 10 s wide and readable, the problem visible within 3 s |
| Problem → fix pairs | 4–6 pairs | Each problem is caused by the viewer's own last fix; each fix names one part |
| The mechanism | once | One named mechanism that makes the machine work |
| Payoff | at 83–92 % of length | It works: lights on, water flows, the reveal |
| Ending | last line | Hands the viewer the credit, in our own words |
Rules: no science terms in the first 20 s; never two named terms back to back; something new and big every two sentences; **nothing just appears: every object arrives by a process** (dug, poured, lowered, bolted).
Two sound versions: (a) build chain with a music bed and ~one real sound per action, ~4.1 w/s; (b) voice-only cutaway with the background >43 dB below the voice, 3.2–3.5 w/s.
